Hit Analyzer 2 Help

Hit Analyzer 2 uses a webcam to detect a laser spot, register hits, and show where they landed on a calibrated target. Open Help or press F1 to return to this guide.

Record your first hit

  1. Connect your webcam and point it at the target so the shooting area is visible in the preview.
  2. Open Camera and select the webcam. If it is missing from the list, click Refresh webcams. Choose a camera mode if the list is available, set the preview orientation, and click Save. On Linux, see camera mode detection if the mode list is empty.
  3. Open Weapon and select a profile. Choose FirstSpot for a test with a short laser pulse.
  4. Select and calibrate the target. Then open Shooting and click Reset shooting to start from zero.
  5. Fire a test pulse at the target. A hit mapped to the calibrated target increases the Hits counter. If the counter stays at zero, follow the missed hits checklist.

Calibrate the target

  1. Set the camera, resolution, and flip orientation before calibrating. Keep the camera and target fixed while shooting.
  2. Open Target and select the profile that matches the physical target. Profiles are loaded from JSON files in Data/Targets under the application folder. Click Reset calibration if you need a centered starting shape.
  3. Drag the center handle to move the entire guide. Drag the four corner handles until the projected circle follows the outer edge of the physical target and its center matches the bullseye. The guide is yellow when the geometry is valid. If it turns cyan, uncross the edges and spread out the corners until the circle returns.
  4. Click Save to save the calibration together with the current camera settings.
  5. Return to Shooting. Test a hit at a known location and check where the marker appears.

The calibration scale is shown in millimeters per pixel when available. Check the calibration again after moving the camera or target. Changing the target profile, camera, camera mode, or flip resets the active calibration. Changes to the calibration geometry clear recorded hits.

Use Calibration zoom to inspect the calibration at 2×, 4× or 8×. Physical distance is the distance to the actual target. Simulated distance sets the distance represented by the target. A Monoscope profile uses a rectangular calibration and reports positions instead of ring scores.

Settings reference

Shooting

Start Session starts a new session using the selected hit limit, time limit and countdown. Reset shooting clears recorded hits and aim paths. Show all hits displays the recorded hits on the target.

The target preview shows the current aim as a red cross with the weapon offset applied. The cross disappears when the camera loses the spot. TimeOut displays the saved path after a hit. External Event displays the path before the hit in yellow and the path after it in cyan.

The readiness circle above Save is gray for Waiting, orange for Tracking, and green for Ready. FirstSpot becomes ready after the detection interval. NewSpot also requires the previous spot to disappear. TimeOut shows Tracking while building the path and Ready once the path meets the duration and allowed-area requirements. External Event shows Tracking when a spot is visible and Ready while an accepted trigger press is active.

Session results show the mean point of impact and the CEP50 (R50) and CEP80 (R80) circles. Both are centered on the mean. Show smallest 80% circle adds the smallest circle covering at least 80% of the recorded hits when available.

Camera

Select a webcam, choose an available resolution and frame rate, and use the flip selector to orient the image. Click Save to keep your changes.

Camera mode detection on Linux

Install the v4l-utils package to enable camera mode detection. Hit Analyzer 2 uses its v4l2-ctl command to list supported resolutions, frame rates, and pixel formats. After installing it, open Camera and click Refresh webcams. The mode list shows supported modes at 640 × 480 or higher and 25 FPS or faster.

Without v4l-utils, the mode selector is unavailable and the camera opens at its default resolution, typically 640 × 480. The exact resolution depends on the camera and driver.

Detection

Simple
Looks for the brightest part of the image that meets the detection threshold.
Blob
Uses the center of the largest connected bright region that meets the minimum area.
Sensitivity
Increase sensitivity to pick up dimmer spots. Decrease it if bright areas in the scene are counted as hits.
Brightness, contrast, and saturation
Adjust the camera image that the detector processes. Check the preview and test a pulse after changing these values.
Show flares
Highlights bright regions that meet the detection threshold so you can see which parts of the image may affect detection.
Show Allowed Area
Outlines the allowed area and hatches the margins on the camera image. In TimeOut mode, the final aim position must fall inside this area to register a hit.

Save in any settings panel saves the current configuration, including the camera, detection, preview, and target settings.

Weapon

The selector displays each profile as its name followed by its filename in parentheses. Profiles are loaded from JSON files in Data/Weapons under the application folder. Selecting a profile applies its saved trigger, interval, and offsets. Click Save to write the changes to that profile and select it for the next launch.

Next detection interval (ms)
Set a delay of 100 to 5,000 milliseconds. See Trigger types for when the delay starts and how it applies.
Weapon offset (px)
Adjust X and Y from −100 to +100 pixels to align the displayed aim with the point of aim. Positive X moves it right and positive Y moves it up. Click Reset to zero both offsets. The live red cross shows the detected laser spot. The cyan circle shows the same point after applying the weapon offset.

Audio

Use Enable audio to turn on sound. Hit sound effect selects a hit sound alone, spoken score, or spoken score with 8- or 12-direction spotter calls.

Effect volume controls hit sounds, spotter calls and session start and end sounds. SGA volume independently controls sound guided aiming. Both range from 0 (muted) to 100. The default of 50 uses the normal playback level at the current system volume.

Sound guided aiming (SGA) works with TimeOut and External Event on scored targets. The tone rises with the score and becomes quieter as the aim moves farther outside the scoring rings. Switching to another trigger turns SGA off.

With Stereo guidance, Focus on Aim makes the tone louder on the side of the detected aim and Focus on Target makes it louder toward the target center. Turning off stereo sends the same tone to both channels. The Audio preview shows the current SGA aim as a red cross and indicates the sound direction with speaker icons.

Click Save to keep your audio settings. Advanced tone settings are in Audio.SGA in settings.json. They include the three frequencies, two pulse periods, quiet-channel gain and duty cycle. Restart after editing the file.

AppLink

Use AppLink to connect compatible software through HIP. Set the receive and destination ports. Advanced settings exposes the local and destination addresses and application SID. Use Send test message to check the connection, then click Save. Monoscope hits are sent as target coordinates when HIP is enabled.

Trigger types

FirstSpot
Registers a hit whenever a spot is detected and the interval since the last hit has elapsed. A spot that stays visible can register repeatedly.
NewSpot
Registers a hit when a spot appears after the interval since the last hit. If it appears too early and stays visible, let it disappear and try again after the interval.
TimeOut
Tracks the spot while you aim. After the spot disappears and the aim path times out, the last position registers if the path meets the configured minimum duration and the final aim position is inside the allowed area. The included configuration uses a minimum path duration of 1.5 seconds and a timeout of 400 milliseconds without a detected spot.
External Event
With the main window active, press and hold T long enough for the camera to capture the visible spot. Each accepted press can register one hit. The interval starts at the accepted press, even if it yields no hit. Release T and wait for the interval before pressing again.

Troubleshooting

No camera preview

Check the camera connection, close other apps that may be using the webcam, and click Refresh webcams. Select the camera again and try another available camera mode if the preview stays blank.

Camera resolution or frame rate is unavailable on Linux

If the mode selector says Could not load camera modes, check that v4l-utils is installed and refresh the camera list.

The counter misses hits

  1. Check that the preview shows the entire shooting area and that the laser spot is inside the frame.
  2. Open Target and check the calibration guides. A hit needs valid calibration to be included in the counter.
  3. Turn on Show flares. Increase sensitivity a little at a time and repeat the test. If the background starts lighting up, reduce glare on the target and test again.
  4. If Blob misses a small spot, try Simple and compare the counter after a test pulse.
  5. Check the selected trigger type. With NewSpot, let the spot disappear and wait for the interval before firing again. With TimeOut, turn on Show Allowed Area and check the final aim position. With External Event, check that the main window is active and that the spot is visible while you hold T.

The counter increases without a shot

Use Show flares to locate bright reflections or background lights in the preview. Remove them from the frame or lower sensitivity, then fire another test pulse.

Hits appear in the wrong place on the target

Open Target and check the projected circle and center against the physical target. If the calibration lines up but every hit is shifted in the same direction, check the selected profile's Weapon offset (px).
